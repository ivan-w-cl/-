## L06.S03.B01: Explain the Choice, Not Just the Action

Scene family: standalone_fictional_decision

### L06.S03.c001 · coach

An account of an action becomes more useful when the listener can understand the choice behind it. Explain what you knew, what was uncertain and why that uncertainty mattered, without turning a decision into proof of a successful outcome.

**Visual:** Open a formal before-decision information board, with action and result kept in distinct lanes. Do not impose a compulsory answer formula or show a success score.

**Phrase anchor:** the choice behind it

## L06.S03.B02: Connect the Uncertainty to the Decision

### L06.S03.c004 · coach

## L06.S03.B04: Separate a Reason from an Outcome

Scene family: shared_source_based_analysis

### L06.S03.c010 · coach

The shared facts support a simple decision explanation: the labels conflicted, their relationship was uncertain, and checking avoided an unverified choice. The later confirmation allowed a specific update. None of that establishes a measured improvement for all visitors.

**Visual:** All routes rejoin once. Rebuild before-check and after-confirmation facts directly from the authoritative case, independent of viewed or skipped branch content.

**Phrase anchor:** None of that establishes a measured improvement

### L06.S03.c011 · coach

A reason can include a concern about what might happen. Say could confuse visitors when that is the stated concern. Do not change it into visitors were confused unless the account actually contains that observation.

**Visual:** Compare possible confusion with an explicitly unsupported past-event wording. Never animate the latter as a case event. Preserve the actual one-visitor observation in its separate lane.

**Phrase anchor:** unless the account actually contains that observation
