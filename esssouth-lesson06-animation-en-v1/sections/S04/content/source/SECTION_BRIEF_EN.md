## Exact script and scene map

## L06.S04.B01: Separate What Changed From What You Conclude

Scene family: formal_results_map

### L06.S04.c001 · coach

An account of an experience often includes what happened afterwards. The useful question is not how impressive you can make that result sound, but what you actually know. Separate the change you made, what you observed, and conclusions that would need more evidence.

**Visual:** Open a formal three-lane evidence board without characters. Keep action, observation and inference visually distinct; no score or impact meter.

**Phrase anchor:** what you actually know

### L06.S04.c002 · coach

Return to our fictional visitor-guide example. The participant changed the map label to Blue Room and told Maya and Noah. Maya checked the schedule, and Noah assembled the updated guide. These are completed actions in the case, with different people responsible for different parts.

**Visual:** Display Fictional worked example throughout. Show F06 as a role map, not a memory scene with invented dialogue; retain all three named responsibilities.

**Phrase anchor:** different people responsible

### L06.S04.c003 · coach

We also know one observation from the event: the participant saw one visitor use the map to find Blue Room. That supports describing this particular use of the map. It is not a total count of people helped, or a measure of the whole event’s success.

**Visual:** Show the single F07 observation as a labelled evidence card. If a schematic person is used, show exactly one observed visitor, no crowd, congratulatory reaction, route duration or invented dialogue.

**Phrase anchor:** this particular use of the map

### L06.S04.h101 · hold 8 s

Privately distinguish the completed guide update from the single observed use.

**Visual:** Keep the action and observation cards apart. Do not fill a learner answer or infer understanding.

## L06.S04.B02: Say the Result at the Level the Evidence Supports

Scene family: brief_account_and_analysis

### L06.S04.d001 · learner

I changed the map label and told Maya and Noah. Maya checked the schedule, and Noah assembled the updated guide. During the event, I saw one visitor use the map to find Blue Room.

**Visual:** Brief case-participant model labelled Fictional account. Preserve attribution, and do not put this text inside a real learner’s response area.

**Phrase anchor:** I saw one visitor

### L06.S04.c004 · coach

Notice the difference between I saw and everyone could. The first reports the available observation. The second would make a wider claim that this case cannot support. Specific limits do not erase a contribution; they help the listener understand its actual scope.

**Visual:** Underline the exact I saw phrase. Place Everyone could find the room in a separate Unsupported claim example frame, never as a historical quote.

**Phrase anchor:** this case cannot support

### L06.S04.c005 · coach

There was no visitor survey or total count of people helped. Avoid turning the single observation into a percentage, an estimate of many visitors, or a claim that nobody was confused. None of those details is available in the example.

**Visual:** Keep F08 visible. Do not animate invented bars, visitor totals, survey responses, percentages or extra successful people, even as positive decorative context.

**Phrase anchor:** None of those details is available

## L06.S04.B03: A Sequence Is Not a Causal Comparison

Scene family: reason_and_result

### L06.S04.c006 · coach

The label was changed before the observed visitor used the map. But we do not know what would have happened without the change. That visitor might have found the room anyway. The case does not provide a comparison that can settle the question.

**Visual:** Use a factual sequence rail: label updated, then observed map use. Keep the counterfactual as an empty question space, not an animated alternate event or a causal proof arrow.

**Phrase anchor:** without the change

### L06.S04.c007 · coach

You can still explain the decision. Conflicting labels could confuse visitors, so the participant checked with the coordinator instead of guessing. That is the reasoning behind the action. It does not establish that confusion was measured, or that the change prevented it for everyone.

**Visual:** Separate stated reasoning from observed result. Show the coordinator confirmation only as the given indirect fact; invent neither exact quotations nor the time taken.

**Phrase anchor:** the reasoning behind the action

### L06.S04.d002 · learner

I wanted the map label to match the door sign. I saw one visitor use the map to find the room, but I cannot say what would have happened without the change.

**Visual:** A second short fictional model. Keep intention, observation and uncertainty as distinct cards; do not add a new event or a claim that the participant inspected the door personally.

**Phrase anchor:** cannot say what would have happened

### L06.S04.c008 · coach

This account preserves the contribution without making the participant responsible for the whole event. It also leaves room for other people’s work. You do not need an invented achievement to explain why checking an ambiguous detail was a sensible action in this case.

**Visual:** Return to the three-role map and the limited evidence card. No leader appointment, award, event-success badge or generic hero composition.

**Phrase anchor:** other people’s work

## L06.S04.B04: Check One Result Sentence

Scene family: private_retrieval_and_close

### L06.S04.c009 · coach

Privately form a result sentence from the case facts, or check one from a real experience of your own. Which words report an observation, and which might claim more? Keep anything unknown visible rather than filling the gap with a number or a confident guess.

**Visual:** Provide the fictional fact reference separately from a blank private area. Offer own truthful experience or labelled fiction; do not collect, verify or generate a personal account.

**Phrase anchor:** claim more

### L06.S04.h102 · hold 12 s

Privately review the scope of one result sentence.

**Visual:** No model is inserted into the private area; no automatic correction or performance inference.

### L06.S04.c010 · coach

A precise result can be modest. State what changed, give the observation you can support, and distinguish any wider uncertainty. These are useful checks when needed, not three compulsory sentences. Next we will connect reflection to a specific follow-up question.

**Visual:** End on optional evidence checks with no numbered formula, official rubric, fixed answer duration or mastery indicator.

**Phrase anchor:** not three compulsory sentences
