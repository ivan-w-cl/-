A parent asks: my kid is sitting an extra university interview on top of the gaokao. Does it actually change anything?

Let's open the file.

Exhibit A: XJTLU's 2026 brochure for Jiangsu. The composite score is out of 100.

The gaokao, scaled to 100, counts for 65 percent. The university's own interview counts for 35.

Now a hypothetical reconstruction. Student A: 600 on the gaokao, 90 in the interview. Student B: 615 and 70.

Scaled from 750, A comes to 83.50. B comes to 77.80.

B led by fifteen gaokao points, and still finishes behind.

Finding one: the interview is points you can prepare for.

Two caveats. This example predicts nothing about real admissions.

And your gaokao score must first clear the special-category control line.

Exhibits B and C. XJTLU: if you're not admitted this way, you can still apply in the regular undergraduate round.

CUHK-Shenzhen: it doesn't affect your later applications.

Finding two: one more chance to be admitted.

But check the jurisdiction.

In Guangdong, the same university uses 65 for the gaokao, 30 for the interview, 5 for academic tests, and the deadline moves to May 20th.

Always check the province and the year.

It also costs time: applications in April and May, and the interview soon after the gaokao.

Exhibit D, on the interview: it assesses English, overall competence and ability, not subject knowledge.

So the skill to practise is simple: a view, and the reason behind it.

Write only an opinion, and the feedback flags the missing reason. Add because, and that flag clears.

Verdict: one more assessment, and one more chance, paid for with a threshold, time and preparation.

Before you apply, check three things: your province, the control line, and your time to prepare. Case closed.
