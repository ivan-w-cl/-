## L08.S02.B02: Do Not Use Silence as a Signature

Scene family: status_distinctions

### L08.S02.c004 · coach

A person who has not replied has not supplied a view you can report. Nodding, looking at the board or letting a summary pass might have several meanings. If the status matters, ask a focused question and leave room for a correction or a request for more time.

**Visual:** Display No stated view separately from Supports and Does not support. Neutral character gestures never move a status card.

**Phrase anchor:** has not supplied a view

### L08.S02.c005 · coach

You can also overcorrect. Saying nothing is agreed can erase a real point of support. Keep agreement at the level where it exists: a named person's support for a named element, with the words that limit it. A complete group decision requires more evidence than a tidy diagram.

**Visual:** Zoom by card movement, not camera, from whole-plan label to individual supported elements. Use status text as well as colour.

**Phrase anchor:** at the level where it exists

### L08.S02.c006 · coach

The prompt gives a firm constraint: at least five minutes for questions within thirty. That is not something you have to infer from a person's reaction. Separate the task requirement from the group's preferences so the summary can preserve both without calling them the same kind of agreement.

**Visual:** Use a distinct fixed requirements rail beneath the support ledger.

**Phrase anchor:** Separate the task requirement

### L08.S02.c007 · coach

A useful summary can therefore contain a positive connection and an open question together. It need not sound indecisive. Saying what is supported gives the group a base to work from; saying what remains open tells them what their next decision actually concerns.

**Visual:** Build an accurate-summary card from Supported elements plus Remaining decision; do not fill the learner response field.

**Phrase anchor:** a positive connection and an open question

### L08.S02.c008 · coach

Now compare three possible accounts of this same baseline. Judge each against the actual statements, not just against how confident or friendly it sounds. The question is whether the account changes the scope of support or discards useful information.

**Visual:** Prepare three neutral option cards; all keep the same ledger visible.

**Phrase anchor:** the scope of support

### L08.S02.c009 · coach

Choose the account you could defend by pointing back to the source. Afterward, notice whether a peer's reply confirms only their own position or settles something wider. A reply from one person is still one person's reply.

**Visual:** Hold the source names beside the choice area; no winning colour before selection.

**Phrase anchor:** one person is still one person

### L08.S02.c011 · coach
