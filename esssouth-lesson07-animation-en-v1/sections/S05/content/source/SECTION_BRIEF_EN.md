Scene family: fresh_sidetrack_excerpt

### L07.S05.c001 · coach

A discussion can drift toward an interesting detail while leaving its task unfinished. Before calling the new topic irrelevant, ask what connection it has to the decision. Sometimes a detail matters; sometimes the group needs to return to a more immediate question.

**Visual:** Open on the task and a new-topic card. Neither is labelled good or bad; make the missing relationship visible.

**Phrase anchor:** what connection it has to the decision

## L07.S05.B02: Ask About the Link

Scene family: relevance_test

### L07.S05.d002 · learner

How would choosing the lettering help new members meet or find out about the drawing group?

**Visual:** An authored relevance question directed to the content, not a challenge to Noah’s competence. Do not immediately supply a favourable or hostile peer reaction.

**Phrase anchor:** help new members meet

### L07.S05.c004 · coach

This question gives the idea a chance to earn its place. A clear link could change your judgement. Without that link, the group may spend its discussion comparing styles while the required activity is still undefined. Keep the task, rather than personal taste, as the reference.

**Visual:** Show possible relation and absent relation as alternatives to examine. Do not claim a measured amount of discussion time has already been lost.

**Phrase anchor:** A clear link could change your judgement

### L07.S05.c005 · coach

For example, legibility might matter if an introduction activity uses names written on paper. That is a possible task-related design question, not a fact about the current proposal. Redesigning the centre’s sign is a different issue unless someone explains how it serves this activity.

**Visual:** Place the names-on-paper example inside a clearly labelled Hypothetical connection frame. Use only existing paper and pens; do not invent printed cards, purchased materials, an adopted activity or a real redesign request.

**Phrase anchor:** a possible task-related design question

### L07.S05.c006 · coach

**Visual:** End the first example with its relevance question unanswered. Return to the formal teaching board. ## L07.S05.B03: Return to Something Specific

Scene family: worked_redirection

### L07.S05.d003 · learner

**Visual:** Show a separate conditional alternative from the reset lettering-question baseline. Persist the heading Alternative: if the task link is still unclear. **Phrase anchor:** We still need that link

### L07.S05.c007 · coach

The return is specific: it names the missing link. Simply saying focus, or repeating that the group is off topic, gives less help about what to do next. A useful redirection makes the task easier to pick up again without deciding the answer for everyone.

**Visual:** Compare a vague Focus card with the precise unresolved question. No character shaming or full plan appears.

**Phrase anchor:** without deciding the answer for everyone

### L07.S05.c008 · coach

Remember what the twenty minutes means. It limits the welcome activity being planned, not each person’s turn or the time available for this discussion. You can point to an unfinished task without inventing a countdown or claiming that the group is already late.

**Visual:** Place 20 minutes only on the proposed activity boundary. Keep discussion and speaking-turn areas free of numerical deadlines or invented urgency.

**Phrase anchor:** not each person’s turn
