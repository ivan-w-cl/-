## Exact script and scene map

## L08.S04.B01: Use the Same Questions for Both Options

Scene family: shared_criteria

### L08.S04.c001 · coach

When two arrangements compete, compare them against the same questions. In this taster, will newcomers have an opportunity to discuss the text? How will they find out about the club? Can the stages and transitions fit within thirty minutes while preserving the minimum question time? These questions come from this task, not a universal ranking system.

**Visual:** Open two candidate columns under one shared criterion row. Do not assign numerical quality scores.

**Phrase anchor:** the same questions

### L08.S04.c002 · coach

One candidate gives more room to a demonstration before pairs begin. Another keeps the example brief and leaves more room for participants to try. Neither description supplies exact durations yet. Compare the likely purpose and the possible cost, without treating those expectations as measured visitor outcomes.

**Visual:** Use flexible duration bands, not invented minute allocations. Both columns remain Candidate; expectations use may/could.

**Phrase anchor:** possible cost

### L08.S04.c003 · coach

A longer example may make the reasoning easier to inspect. It can also use minutes that would otherwise go to pairs, sharing or questions. You cannot preserve every stage at full size simply by calling the arrangement balanced. The total stays the same even when each element has a good reason.

**Visual:** One fixed30-minute container; optional bands expand/contract without implying a real chosen timetable.

**Phrase anchor:** The total stays the same

### L08.S04.h101 · hold 8 s

Privately name a benefit one candidate is intended to offer and a cost it could create.

**Visual:** Blank benefit/cost notes. Show no computed winner.

## L08.S04.B02: Make the Reason Inspectable

Scene family: brief_tradeoff_example

### L08.S04.c004 · coach

A useful trade-off statement connects an arrangement to the goal and names its limitation. You can prefer the briefer example because the task asks newcomers to try discussing the text. That preference still needs a check that the example is clear enough and that the pair task has a useful starting point.

**Visual:** Build a provisional preference card with purpose and check attached; preserve the need to try rather than only watch.

**Phrase anchor:** names its limitation

### L08.S04.d001 · learner

I would keep the example brief so there is room to try the discussion in pairs. We still need to check that it shows the reasoning clearly.

**Visual:** Participant gives a concise preference and its limitation; no other character automatically agrees.

**Phrase anchor:** shows the reasoning clearly

### L08.S04.c005 · coach

That statement does not claim a short example is always better. It explains why this candidate might fit this task. Someone can now question the link, suggest a clearer example or ask whether sharing needs a different place in the sequence. Those are useful revisions, not proof that the first speaker failed.

**Visual:** The same card remains editable; possible challenges attach to its reasoning rather than a character score.

**Phrase anchor:** this candidate might fit this task

### L08.S04.d002 · noah

I want the example to include a reason, not only an interpretation. Could we check that before deciding how brief it can be?

**Visual:** Noah offers an authored check in this excerpt; it does not establish a new source fact about the eventual demonstration.

**Phrase anchor:** include a reason

### L08.S04.c006 · coach

That question helps define what must survive if the example is shortened. Keeping the reasoning visible is different from choosing the duration now. The group can look at a proposed example, check its essential content, and then consider how it affects the available time.

**Visual:** Separate essential content from duration decision. No actual completed demonstration or measured length appears.

**Phrase anchor:** what must survive

## L08.S04.B03: Protect a Requirement without Making a Promise

Scene family: limits_and_unknowns

### L08.S04.c007 · coach

Apply the same care to the question period. At least five minutes is required; it is not evidence that five minutes will answer everything. If questions need longer, another part must fit into less time. Preserve the requirement while being honest about what the information sheet can answer.

**Visual:** Minimum-time label separate from answer-coverage label. Unknown visitor questions remain unresolved.

**Phrase anchor:** not evidence that five minutes will answer everything

### L08.S04.c008 · coach

Do not create a false choice between respect and selection. Taking someone's contribution seriously can mean preserving its purpose in a different form. It can also mean explaining why it does not fit this version. Respect does not require keeping every proposed detail unchanged.

**Visual:** Carry an attributed purpose card into a revised arrangement; if set aside, keep a small reason note, not a discarded-person icon.

**Phrase anchor:** preserving its purpose in a different form

### L08.S04.c009 · coach

A trade-off remains open to review when you say which assumption it depends on. Here, a brief example depends on someone being able and willing to provide it. If that is not established, the next step is to check or revise the candidate, not to announce that the arrangement is ready.

**Visual:** Attach a willingness/preparation check to the demonstration. No assigned role badge is generated.

**Phrase anchor:** which assumption it depends on

### L08.S04.h102 · hold 12 s

Privately express one task-linked preference with its cost or unresolved condition.

**Visual:** Leave the response area blank. Show the criterion headings as optional prompts, not a required sentence formula.

## L08.S04.B04: Distinguish a Reasoned Preference from a Decision

Scene family: status_bridge

### L08.S04.c010 · coach

You can now explain a preference without presenting it as the group's decision. Keep the proposal, its purpose, its cost and its unresolved checks close together. A listener should not have to recover the caveat from a much earlier part of the discussion.

**Visual:** Create one compact candidate card with nearby conditions; keep a clearly visible Not adopted tag.

**Phrase anchor:** without presenting it as the group

### L08.S04.c011 · coach

Next, bring the candidate back to the group and ask about the support that matters. A useful check can reveal conditional support, a specific reservation or a need for more information. Each of those responses helps determine the next step without inventing a vote or a final authority.

**Visual:** End on a proposed check addressed to the group; no majority counter or leader crown.

**Phrase anchor:** the support that matters
