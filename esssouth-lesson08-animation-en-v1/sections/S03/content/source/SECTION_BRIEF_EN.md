## L08.S03.B01: Look for a Connection, Not Just a List

Scene family: coherent_sequence

### L08.S03.c001 · coach

Combining contributions does not mean giving every suggestion its own full slot. Look for a connection between the jobs they could do. An example might prepare people for a pair task; sharing might let the group compare something that came from that task. That is a sequence with a reason, rather than a list of favourites.

## L08.S03.B02: Keep the Whole Inside the Boundary

Scene family: time_and_dependency

### L08.S03.c004 · coach

Thirty minutes is the total activity time. At least five of those minutes must remain for questions. That leaves at most twenty-five for everything else, including transitions, and less if questions receive longer. Twenty-five is a limit to respect, not a schedule the group has already chosen.

**Visual:** Use one 30-minute outline with a flexible question region of minimum5. The rest is labelled At most25 including transitions, not an agreed25-minute allocation.

**Phrase anchor:** including transitions

### L08.S03.c005 · coach

An attractive combination may also depend on a person or an unanswered practical question. Here, someone would have to give the example. We have not established who is willing or what preparation they need. Keep that check attached to the stage instead of hiding it behind the word demonstration.

**Visual:** Attach a Who would give it? open tag to the example stage; do not assign a character or draw an available expert.

**Phrase anchor:** Keep that check attached

### L08.S03.c006 · coach

This does not mean you must solve every detail before suggesting a connection. You can propose a candidate and name the checks needed to judge it. The difference is between a plausible direction and a plan that has already been timed, resourced and adopted.

**Visual:** Show Candidate to develop with open timing/person tags. No stamp claiming feasible or verified.

**Phrase anchor:** a plausible direction

### L08.S03.c007 · coach

Some ideas cannot be combined unchanged. They may compete for the same minutes, rely on different assumptions, or duplicate a job. Explain the clash instead of treating the person who offered an idea as the problem. An element can be shortened, linked differently or set aside with a reason.

**Visual:** Cards conflict at a time-boundary rail, not at character faces. Show optional revisions without committing to one.

**Phrase anchor:** Explain the clash

### L08.S03.c008 · coach

## L08.S03.B03: Keep a Candidate Revisable

Scene family: candidate_review

### L08.S03.c010 · coach

Whatever you chose, inspect both the connection and its limits. The parts need related purposes, the whole must respect the task, and unresolved checks should remain visible. Those tests help you develop a proposal; they do not prove how visitors will respond or whether the group will accept it.

**Visual:** Common rejoin uses source goals and open checks, independent of branch replies. No result or consensus invented.

**Phrase anchor:** both the connection and its limits

### L08.S03.h102 · hold 12 s

**Visual:** Two blank private notes alongside the task; no completed template or score.

## L08.S03.B04: Prepare to Explain the Trade-off

Scene family: tradeoff_bridge

### L08.S03.c011 · coach

A workable proposal is not a promise to keep every element at its original size. If one part grows, something else may have to change. Make that consequence explicit so the group can compare a real trade-off rather than approve several incompatible promises.

**Visual:** Grow one stage only within a fixed total; adjacent space contracts. Label the movement Illustrative allocation, not chosen schedule.

**Phrase anchor:** a real trade-off

### L08.S03.c012 · coach

The next step is to explain why a particular connection fits the shared aims, and what it gives up. That lets others question your reasoning or offer a better arrangement without first having to undo an exaggerated claim of agreement.

**Visual:** End on a candidate with reason and cost cards, both provisional.

**Phrase anchor:** what it gives up
