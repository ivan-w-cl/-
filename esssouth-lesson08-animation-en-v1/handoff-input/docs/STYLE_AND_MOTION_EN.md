# Accepted visual language

Preserve the supplied reference's light gray/white stage, restrained blue/teal accents, readable dark type, fixed camera, card geometry and character artwork. This is a continuation, not a redesign. The reference remains unchanged; write explicit new scene adapters for L08.

Formal animated explanation carries the lesson. Characters appear for brief useful examples, then leave as reasoning returns to the board. Do not keep three people idle throughout a thirty-minute lecture. Do not turn whole paragraphs into slides; use the short labels and each cue's visualEn to stage one meaningful distinction at a time.

## Motion that explains synthesis

Keep a contribution's name and identity attached as it moves. An attributed proposal can join a candidate sequence, but its motion must not silently imply approval. Preserve visible conditions and open questions near the item they qualify. Changes of support occur after the exact authored words that support them.

Use a persistent candidate card, a support ledger and a separate requirements rail. Proposed, conditional support, open and adopted must differ by words and structure, not colour alone. No final adopted state is supplied in this lesson. A group-consensus badge, approval animation, voting tally or leader crown would invent meaning.

When S05 changes the snapshot, show that change explicitly. When a Practice branch resets, remove its temporary speech/reactions and restore the original evidence. Never let a previous branch's answer or an unselected model appear through a transition. In S06, do not morph the learner's blank space into the model response.

Do not add camera movement, continuous bouncing, score effects, generic title spins or rhythmic mouth flapping. Without real narration, mouths stay closed. Retain small anticipation, staggered listener reactions and settled poses when they clarify the example.

## Text, layout and devices

Text remains editable with explicit slots, wrapping, minimum sizes and overflow warnings. Keep exact scripts separate from short on-screen labels. Long options and the S06 model need deliberately sized layouts; do not shrink text to a barely readable size, crop it or silently ellipsize its conditions.

Check settled frames and movement frames, including status tags against vector items such as clocks and rails. A text-only layout test can miss those collisions. Review the actual painted frames as well as geometry; transparent bounding-box contact alone is not evidence of an unreadable overlap.

Test 1.3x/1.6x text expansion, a long unbroken token and fallback fonts. On phones, keep normal-size DOM captions and stacked choices outside the small 16:9 illustration. Aim for 44 CSS-pixel controls. Check tablet portrait/landscape and state whether tests are simulated or physical. Reduced motion must preserve all information and reading opportunities.
