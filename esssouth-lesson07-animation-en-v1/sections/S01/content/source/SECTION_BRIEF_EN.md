## L07.S01.B01: Notice before Intervening

Scene family: task_context_and_observation

### L07.S01.c001 · coach

When a discussion slows down, the next useful move is not always to speak more. First notice what is happening and what the group still needs. A short pause, an unfinished explanation and two overlapping turns call for different judgements.

**Visual:** Open with three separate schematic excerpts: a pause, an unfinished link and two turn lanes. Label them examples rather than a simultaneous real event. Keep the formal teaching board dominant.

**Phrase anchor:** different judgements

### L07.S01.c002 · coach

Use this practice task. The activity’s twenty-minute limit is a condition of the plan, not a timer for the discussion. No recommendation has been adopted and no activity has taken place.

**Visual:** Establish Practice example before the source prompt. Show a planning-time card, not a discussion countdown, and leave the selected activity empty.

**Phrase anchor:** not a timer for the discussion

### L07.S01.d001 · prompt_reader

A community centre wants a 20-minute welcome activity for new members. Help them meet one another and find out about its weekly drawing group. Your group should recommend one activity and explain how it serves both aims. Use the main room and existing paper and pens; do not buy anything.

**Visual:** Display the exact full source prompt with phrase-aligned emphasis. The reader is offstage, with no examiner artwork. Preserve wording and punctuation. This English prompt remains an unvoiced reading slot in the silent handoff and stays English in later localization.

**Phrase anchor:** explain how it serves both aims

## L07.S01.B02: Describe What the Words Establish

Scene family: observable_content_and_open_task

### L07.S01.c003 · coach

Maya has offered paired introductions. Exchanging names and interests could provide a way for people to meet. Her sentence has not yet explained how newcomers would find out about the weekly drawing group. That link remains open.

**Visual:** Connect the proposal conditionally to meeting people. Leave a separate find-out-about-the-group link visibly unresolved. Do not require one opening sentence to solve the entire brief.

**Phrase anchor:** That link remains open

### L07.S01.c004 · coach

An open link is something to develop, not proof that the idea is poor or its speaker is unprepared. A useful next contribution might explore that connection instead of replacing the whole proposal.

**Visual:** Keep Maya’s idea intact while opening one task-link space. No red cross, character judgement, competence score or invented diagnosis.

**Phrase anchor:** not proof that the idea is poor

**Visual:** Retain the exact proposal and both source aims beside a blank private note. No choice, motive or answer is automatically assigned.

**Phrase anchor:** separate a fact from an interpretation

## L07.S01.B03: Do Not Read Motives from a Moment

Scene family: unknown_motive_and_timing

### L07.S01.c006 · coach

A pause does not tell you whether others agree, are thinking, have missed a point or prefer another idea. Those are possibilities. Unless someone provides more information, the cause of that pause remains unknown.

**Visual:** Show one pause with several neutral possible explanations outside the known-facts area. Do not depict a thought bubble as a character’s actual internal state.

**Phrase anchor:** the cause of that pause remains unknown

### L07.S01.c007 · coach

Likewise, beginning at the same moment does not by itself establish hostility. One person speaking less in a short excerpt does not make them the quiet member of the group. Describe the moment rather than assigning a personality.

**Visual:** Use separate timing lanes and a bounded excerpt frame. No angry acting, persistent quiet-person badge or inferred intent.

**Phrase anchor:** rather than assigning a personality

### L07.S01.c008 · coach

Sometimes leaving a little space is useful. Sometimes a focused question helps. There is no single pause length after which someone must take charge, and no need to appoint yourself the leader to make a small contribution.

**Visual:** Keep waiting and a focused question as context-dependent options. Do not introduce a universal seconds threshold, speaking quota or leader hierarchy.

**Phrase anchor:** no single pause length

## L07.S01.B04: Choose a Small Move with a Purpose

Scene family: repair_scope_and_limits

### L07.S01.c009 · coach

Ask what your next words would make easier: understanding an idea, finishing a turn, connecting a proposal to the task, or hearing another view. Choose the immediate need rather than trying to manage everything at once.

**Visual:** Display four optional repair purposes around the open task link. Do not number them as a compulsory sequence or select one for the viewer.

**Phrase anchor:** Choose the immediate need

### L07.S01.c010 · coach

For this proposal, asking how it could introduce the drawing group would bring attention to a missing link. The question would not supply the programme details or prove that the activity works. Those still need to be worked out.

**Visual:** Use a question card pointing to the unresolved find-out aim. Keep programme information and its source unknown; do not invent a facilitator, answer sheet or confirmed programme.

**Phrase anchor:** would not supply the programme details

### L07.S01.c011 · coach

Privately choose one small move for the excerpt. You may leave space or form a focused question. Think about what it would help the group do next, rather than how it would make you appear.

**Visual:** Keep the same excerpt unchanged and a blank private next-move area. No recording, default intervention, performance score or invented response.

**Phrase anchor:** what it would help the group do next

### L07.S01.h102 · hold 8 s

Privately choose one proportionate next move without assuming another person’s motive.

**Visual:** Leave the response blank. Viewers may pause for longer; no model becomes their answer and no participation is inferred.
