## L07.S04.B01: Notice an Opportunity, Not a Personality

Scene family: fresh_comparison_excerpt

### L07.S04.c001 · coach

An invitation can make space for another view. It should not turn a quieter moment into a test that someone has to pass. Start with what you can observe: who has commented on the current question, and what contribution would help the discussion.

**Visual:** Begin on a formal comparison board with a current-question space. Do not show personality labels, confidence meters or a ranking of speaking time.

**Phrase anchor:** what you can observe

**Visual:** Keep the exact source prompt available in a legible reference panel. Show the two aims as shared comparison questions; the 20 minutes belongs to the welcome activity, not speaking turns.

## L07.S04.B03: Avoid Turning Inclusion Into Control

Scene family: invitation_boundary

### L07.S04.c006 · coach

An invitation can also hide an attempt to obtain approval. Maya, you agree with me, right, points toward the answer the speaker wants. Asking what she would add or question leaves room for a different view. The distinction is about the choice you actually offer.

**Visual:** Show the leading question as an explicitly separate illustrative phrase, never a historical quote from the current excerpt. Contrast desired approval with an open contribution.

**Phrase anchor:** a different view

### L07.S04.c007 · coach

Do not keep returning to the same person after they have passed. Give space to the discussion and attend to a later attempt to join. Supporting participation includes making room when someone offers a point, not merely choosing who should speak next.

**Visual:** Show a neutral open discussion space and an optional later contribution marker with no predicted speaker or guaranteed event. No automatic summons or repeated highlight on Maya.

**Phrase anchor:** making room when someone offers a point

### L07.S04.c008 · coach

Equal speaking time is not a rule for every group discussion. Someone might make a brief useful observation; another point may need explanation. Stay attentive to access and relevance without counting compulsory turns or taking authority to allocate everyone’s words.

**Visual:** Use differently sized contribution cards without timers, quotas, leader badges or a universal balanced-turn target.

**Phrase anchor:** without counting compulsory turns
