# L06.S01: Choose One Specific Experience

Outcome: Choose a specific, truthful episode with a clear action or decision to discuss; distinguish a useful experience account from a broad claim about personal qualities, without inventing achievements or disclosing private details.

## Exact script and scene map

## L06.S01.B01: Choose Something You Can Explain

Scene family: experience_scope_and_fictional_frame

### L06.S01.c001 · coach

When asked about an experience, you do not need to begin with your most impressive achievement. Choose something specific that lets you explain what happened, what you did and what you can honestly say about it.

**Visual:** Open with a broad experience-topic card narrowing to one event, action and question. Do not introduce awards, status rankings, an official rubric or a required three-part answer template.

**Phrase anchor:** something specific

### L06.S01.c002 · coach

We will use a fictional worked example, not anyone’s personal record. Three volunteers prepared a one-page visitor guide for a community open afternoon. The participant drew the map, Maya listed session times, and Noah assembled the guide.

**Visual:** Reveal the persistent Fictional worked example frame before any case material. Show three role lanes and one-page guide; these are supplied case facts, not a viewer biography. Maya and Noah need not appear as speaking characters inside the event.

**Phrase anchor:** The participant drew the map

### L06.S01.d001 · learner

I drew the map for a visitor guide. The day before the event, I noticed that my map said Studio B while Maya’s schedule said Blue Room.

**Visual:** Show a brief authored account by the fictional case participant in a separate model frame. Retain the fiction label. This is a later account, not a captured statement from the event or the real viewer.

**Phrase anchor:** Studio B while Maya’s schedule said Blue Room

### L06.S01.c003 · coach

This gives us an episode to examine: conflicting labels while preparing a guide. It is more specific than saying I helped at an event, and it does not yet claim a dramatic result.

**Visual:** Move to a formal board with context and the two exact source labels. Keep the contradiction unresolved until the supplied confirmation facts appear.

**Phrase anchor:** an episode to examine

## L06.S01.B02: Find the Action within the Larger Experience

Scene family: episode_boundary_and_source_facts

### L06.S01.c004 · coach

The participant did not know whether the names meant the same room. Instead of guessing, they asked the coordinator. The coordinator confirmed that both names referred to the same room and that its door sign said Blue Room.

**Visual:** Sequence only the given facts: uncertainty, asking, then confirmation. Use reported information rather than invented direct speech from the coordinator. No new room, timing or conversation details.

**Phrase anchor:** Instead of guessing

### L06.S01.c005 · coach

The participant changed the map label and told Maya and Noah. Maya checked the schedule; Noah assembled the updated guide. That is the shared work surrounding the episode, not evidence that one person completed everything.

**Visual:** Reuse the three role lanes. Put map correction and notification in the participant lane, schedule check in Maya’s, updated assembly in Noah’s. Do not invent additional work or a leader role.

**Phrase anchor:** not evidence that one person completed everything

**Visual:** Retain the labelled source facts and a blank private focus area. No automatic topic selection, personal history capture or model copied into the private area.

**Phrase anchor:** without pretending it happened to you

## L06.S01.B03: Specific Does Not Mean Elaborate

Scene family: relevance_and_information_limits

### L06.S01.c007 · coach

A specific account does not need every detail of the afternoon. Include enough context for the listener to understand the problem and your part. Extra details are useful only when they help explain the point you are making.

**Visual:** Show a compact episode frame within the larger event. Do not populate peripheral cards with invented attendees, decoration, dates or quotations.

**Phrase anchor:** Extra details are useful only when

### L06.S01.c008 · coach

The source also limits the result: the participant saw one visitor use the map to find Blue Room. There was no survey or total count, and no comparison showing what would have happened without the change.

**Visual:** Add the single supplied observation separately from broader unknown impact. Do not animate a crowd succeeding, create a percentage or imply the correction caused the observed visitor’s success.

**Phrase anchor:** one visitor

### L06.S01.c009 · coach

Those limits do not make the experience unusable. The label check is still something the participant can explain. Do not add a larger outcome simply to make the story sound more impressive.

**Visual:** Keep the concrete decision visible alongside the limited observation and unknowns. Avoid a success/failure grade or heroic character treatment.

**Phrase anchor:** Do not add a larger outcome

## L06.S01.B04: Choose a Truthful and Comfortable Topic

Scene family: private_choice_and_alternative

### L06.S01.c010 · coach

For your own practice, consider an ordinary experience you remember well. It could involve preparing something, helping with a task or noticing a problem. Choose an episode small enough to describe clearly, with a part that you can explain from your own experience.

**Visual:** Formal board shows ordinary task categories as possibilities: preparing, helping, noticing. A selection frame narrows to one episode; no achievement badge or invented biography. Keep private response blank.

**Phrase anchor:** small enough

### L06.S01.c011 · coach

If one topic feels too personal, choose another. You can also use the fictional case for language practice, while keeping it labelled as an example. A useful episode does not need an award, a leadership title or a dramatic ending.

**Visual:** Offer two labelled blank paths: Own experience and Fictional example. A small optional privacy note stays in the controls, not a repeated warning card. No automatic answer population.

**Phrase anchor:** A useful episode

### L06.S01.c012 · coach

Privately choose one episode and name the part you could explain. Keep the choice to yourself. What was happening, and where did you take part? Pause here if you would like more time to think.

**Visual:** Private thinking board with empty Setting and My part slots; do not fill them with model events. The fixed reflection hold follows; no recorded response or success mark.

**Phrase anchor:** where did you take part

### L06.S01.h102 · hold 8 s

Privately choose an experience or the explicitly fictional language-practice case.

**Visual:** Keep both possible routes available and the private response blank. Do not assume the viewer has chosen, spoken or disclosed anything.
