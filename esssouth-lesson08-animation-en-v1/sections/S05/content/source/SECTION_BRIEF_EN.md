## L08.S05.B02: Ask a Question That Can Change the Record

Scene family: support_check

### L08.S05.c004 · coach

A focused check asks about an element or a condition that is still open. Do you support this sequence, or is there something you would change? That is more useful than asking everyone to approve a summary that already says everyone agrees. Leave space for a limited or negative answer.

**Visual:** The support ledger opens editable status cells, including conditional and unresolved states, without default selection.

**Phrase anchor:** an element or a condition

**Phrase anchor:** if the demonstration stays brief

**Visual:** Maya row updates to conditional support; timing and leader rows do not change.

## L08.S05.B03: Choose a Next Check without Assigning Authority

Scene family: bounded_next_step

### L08.S05.c007 · coach

The current record has moved forward, but it is still not a final group decision. A useful next step is to draft stage lengths, include transitions and protect the question period, then ask whether the result addresses the reservation. Someone also needs to check who could give the example.

**Visual:** Show updated support ledger beside two unresolved next checks. Do not auto-assign either task or mark them completed.

**Phrase anchor:** still not a final group decision

### L08.S05.c008 · coach

If you offer to make that draft, present it as an offer rather than a command. Nothing in this case appoints a leader or establishes a voting rule. The group can decide how to do the check; the summariser should not quietly claim authority over that decision.

**Visual:** Optional offer card, no leader badge, role assignment or majority tally.

**Phrase anchor:** an offer rather than a command

### L08.S05.d004 · learner

Would it help if we sketched the stage lengths next, including transitions, and then checked whether the sequence fits?

**Visual:** Participant offers a focused next check. No peer reply follows, so acceptance remains unknown.

**Phrase anchor:** including transitions

### L08.S05.c009 · coach

An offer without a reply remains an offer. Keep that distinction when you hand over the summary. Otherwise, a proposed action can quietly become someone's obligation, just as partial support can quietly become consensus.

**Visual:** Next-step card is labelled Offered; do not move it into Assigned or Done.

**Phrase anchor:** remains an offer

## L08.S05.B04: End with an Accurate Handover

Scene family: support_summary

### L08.S05.c010 · coach

A helpful handover lets someone see the candidate, whose support is conditional, what remains unresolved and what check is being proposed. Keep it concise enough to use, but not so short that the conditions disappear. The next speaker should be able to correct one item without rebuilding the whole account.

**Visual:** Compress the ledger into a four-line draft summary, all explicit conditions retained and original sources reachable.

**Phrase anchor:** the conditions disappear

### L08.S05.c011 · coach

The point of checking is not to collect a polite yes. It is to make the group's actual position clear enough for a useful next decision. In the next section, practise giving that kind of summary while keeping your own private rehearsal separate from the authored example.

**Visual:** End with clear partial/open status, then a blank rehearsal card that is not prefilled.

**Phrase anchor:** actual position
